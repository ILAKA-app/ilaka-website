# PRODUCT.md — Ilaka marketing site

register: brand

## Product purpose

Ilaka is a hyperlocal community, events, and class platform for India. It is two-sided:
**organisers** (independent tutors, coaches, studios, event hosts) list their classes and
events, and **neighbours** discover what is happening minutes from home, register, and pay in
the app, with a QR ticket at the door. Free for neighbours; organisers pay a small commission
only when they actually get paid.

The name *ilaka* is the Hindi/Urdu word for **neighbourhood** or **territory**, the few
streets you actually live in.

**This site is a pre-launch / waitlist landing.** The app is under construction and the beta
launches hyperlocal in **Sainikpuri, Hyderabad**, then expands neighbourhood by neighbourhood.
The site's job: explain both sides of Ilaka clearly, make it feel energetic and close-by, and
capture waitlist signups with enough signal (organiser vs. neighbour) to tell the founders who
is showing up.

## Users

- **The Organiser** (runs a business or class): a yoga instructor, run-club lead, tutor, potter,
  or workshop host who wants a full room without living in WhatsApp threads and posters. The
  site's primary hero audience (default headline speaks to them).
- **The Neighbour / Explorer** (18–40, urban India, smartphone-first): wants something to do
  nearby, this week, without joining ten group chats to find it.
- **The Parent** (30–50): looking for tuitions, music, dance, or sport for their child; trust
  and proximity matter.

The waitlist lets a visitor self-identify as **"I run a business/class"** or **"I'm looking
for things to do"**, and the success copy adapts to which they picked.

## Brand voice

Three physical-object words: **poster-bold · sun-warm · plain-spoken.**

Confident and punchy, but warm and neighbourly, never corporate. Short, declarative lines
("Your class deserves a full room.", "Be first in line."). Concrete nouns over abstractions
("a Saturday run club, then filter coffee", not "community fitness experiences"). It reads like
a well-made local poster: bold type, warm paper, one clear thing to do.

Anti-references (what it must NOT sound/look like): generic SaaS landing page; "revolutionise
your neighbourhood experience"; soft blurred-gradient AI hero; cold navy-and-gold fintech;
editorial-magazine display-serif-and-italic reflex.

## Strategic principles

- **Design IS the product here.** The confidence and warmth of the page is the pitch.
- **Two sides, one neighbourhood.** Every fold makes clear this serves both organisers and
  neighbours; the role toggle is the spine of the ask.
- Honest about status: the app is being built and starts in Sainikpuri. Framed as an
  invitation and an early-access offer, not an apology.
- Free-for-neighbours is a headline value, not fine print.
- Every claim maps to something real in the product (discovery, in-app registration + payment,
  QR tickets, following organisers). No invented features.
